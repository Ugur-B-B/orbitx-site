OrbitX for Mac – Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 October 2026. Applies to OrbitX Browser for Mac. The iOS and Windows versions have their own privacy policies.

In short

OrbitX is a web browser for Mac. It has no accounts, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no third-party SDKs. Nothing is sent to the developer, and the developer runs no server that could receive your data. In App Store terms: Data Not Collected. Your history, bookmarks, settings and passwords stay on your Mac. OrbitX does make a few network requests on its own (filter lists, site icons, and optional features you switch on). They go directly from your Mac to the party named below and contain no account, device identifier or usage statistics. This page lists each one.

2. Requests OrbitX makes itself

All of these go straight from your Mac to the named party, over HTTPS, without cookies. The other party necessarily sees your IP address, as with any web request. OrbitX does not check for its own updates; app updates come through the Mac App Store.

3. Websites you visit

When you open a website or search, your browser exchanges data with that site or with your search engine (DuckDuckGo by default), as every browser does. OrbitX's shield blocks many ad and tracker requests. When you download a file, the request to the file's server carries the page's cookies, so that downloads behind a login work; this is ordinary browser behaviour. What a website does with your data is governed by that website's own policy.

4. Camera, microphone and location

OrbitX for Mac does not use the camera, microphone or your location, and the app does not hold the macOS permissions for them. If a website asks for one of them, the request is declined and macOS never shows a permission prompt for OrbitX.

5. What is stored on your Mac

History, bookmarks, shortcuts, open tabs, settings, per-site settings, the list of downloads, site icons, filter lists and saved passwords are stored on your Mac and are never uploaded by OrbitX. There is no sync. OrbitX runs in the macOS App Sandbox: its data lives in the app's own container, ~/Library/Containers/com.orbitx.browsermac, and the app's data folder is excluded from Time Machine backups. Cookies and other website data are kept by WebKit, the web engine of macOS, also within that container.

Files you download are saved to your Downloads folder. Bookmark export, password backup and import from another browser use the standard macOS open and save dialogs: OrbitX can read or write only the file you pick there.

Saved passwords

Saved passwords are encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The key is created on your Mac and kept in your macOS Keychain (the login keychain). Viewing or filling a password asks you to authenticate with Touch ID or your Mac password. Passwords are offered only on the exact website (origin) where they were saved. If you export a backup, the file is encrypted with a password you choose; where you keep that file is up to you. A copied password is removed from the clipboard after 30 seconds.

Private tabs

Private tabs do not save history, passwords or tabs. Cookies and site data from private tabs are kept in memory only, shared only between private tabs, and discarded when you close all private tabs or quit the app. Files you download stay on your Mac.

Deleting your data

Use Settings > Privacy and security > "Clear browsing data", or delete individual entries in History, Bookmarks, Downloads and the password vault. Moving the app to the Trash does not by itself remove its container; to remove all data, also delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.orbitx.browsermac.

6. Legal basis and your rights

The requests in section 2 are needed to provide the features you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); the optional ones are off until you switch them on. Since the developer holds no personal data about you, requests for access, correction or deletion would normally have nothing to act on. You may nevertheless contact the address above at any time. You have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection authority, for example the Landesbeauftragte für den Datenschutz und die Informationsfreiheit Rheinland-Pfalz.

7. Children

OrbitX is a general-purpose browser and is not directed to children. It does not knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.

8. Changes
